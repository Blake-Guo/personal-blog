# Research claims

Use this reference when a paper or benchmark result motivates a post or supports its argument. Validate the claim before building the introduction around it.

## Verify the actual comparison

Read the primary paper, including the relevant figure or table caption and experimental details. For each result, record:

- Authors, paper version, and date.
- Model and reasoning or inference settings.
- Dataset scope, including public versus held-out tasks.
- Metric, units, and the reported before/after values.
- What the baseline already includes.
- Everything changed by the intervention, including tools, prompts, limits, observations, or model-access interfaces.

A configuration with text notes already has a form of memory. Adding stored visual frames and inspection tools evaluates that added setup. If automatic frame delivery changes too, the comparison does not isolate storage alone. Similar care applies to any ablation with several changes.

## Match the wording to the evidence

Use “the authors report” for results established by the paper. Distinguish verification of the reported numbers from independent reproduction of the experiment.

Describe a combined harness improvement as a combined improvement. Attribute a gain to one component only when the experiment isolates it. A result on public games does not establish performance on an entire benchmark's hidden evaluation or general model capability.

Preserve the author's broader opinion as opinion. A specific study can motivate that viewpoint without proving the whole thesis. Keep necessary qualifications close to the result and brief; omit unrelated study details that do not change the interpretation.

Check the introduction, captions, tables, and conclusion for stronger versions of the same claim. Link the exact paper version and supporting section or figure. Respect the applicable quotation and source-use limits.
